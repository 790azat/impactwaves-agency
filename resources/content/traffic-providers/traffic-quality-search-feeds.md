In search feed monetization, **traffic quality** decides everything. It affects your revenue per click, whether revenue is paid out in full and whether you keep your account at all. This guide explains what feed partners look for and how to keep your traffic on the right side of the line.

## Why traffic quality matters so much

Advertisers pay for clicks from real people who might become customers. Ad networks protect them by filtering invalid clicks and scoring traffic sources. When a source sends low-value or invalid clicks, the network reduces what it pays, and the feed partner passes that on.

For you, low quality shows up as:

- **Lower RPC**, because the network discounts traffic it does not trust
- **Clawbacks**, where revenue already reported is deducted after review
- **Blocked sources or closed accounts** in serious or repeated cases

## Signals feed partners monitor

Every partner has its own systems, but most look at similar signals:

| Signal | What healthy looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Click-through rate on the page | Consistent with the page type and topic, without sudden spikes |
| Conversion signals from advertisers | Clicks that lead to actions on advertiser sites |
| Time and behavior on page | Visitors who read and scroll before they click |
| Device and browser mix | A natural mix that matches the traffic source |
| Geography | Visitors from the countries you report, with no hidden regions |
| Source transparency | Every traffic source declared and trackable |

## What counts as bad traffic

- **Bots and automated clicks** of any kind
- **Incentivized clicks**, where users are rewarded for clicking
- **Accidental clicks** from layouts that place search links where users tap by mistake
- **Misleading ads** that promise something the page does not deliver
- **Undeclared sources**, such as buying traffic from a network you did not tell the partner about
- **Pop-unders and forced redirects** unless the partner explicitly allows them

Some of these are obviously against the rules. Others, like an aggressive layout or a clickbait headline, can creep in during testing. Both cost money.

## How to keep traffic clean

1. **Declare every source.** Tell your feed partner which platforms and networks you use, and ask before adding new ones.
2. **Pass tracking parameters.** Use channel or sub IDs so revenue and quality can be traced back to each campaign.
3. **Review page layout.** Search terms should be clearly labeled and placed where users choose to click them.
4. **Match ads to pages.** The page should deliver what the ad promised, in the same language and for the same country.
5. **Watch daily reports.** A sudden rise in page CTR or a drop in RPC is an early warning. Pause the source and investigate before scaling.
6. **Filter at the source.** Use platform exclusions and placement blocklists to cut low-quality apps and sites.

## If your quality score drops

Do not keep scaling and hope it passes. Pause the affected campaigns, compare the problem days with your source data and share your findings with the feed partner. Partners are far more willing to work with teams that react quickly and transparently.
