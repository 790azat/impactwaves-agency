Media buying is a numbers game. Creative and targeting get the attention, but the teams that scale are the ones that read their metrics correctly and act on them every day. This guide covers the core metrics, how to calculate them and what each one tells you.

## Cost metrics

**CPM (cost per mille)** is what you pay for 1,000 ad impressions. It reflects competition for your audience and how the platform rates your ad.

> CPM = Spend / Impressions x 1,000

**CTR (click-through rate)** is the share of impressions that turn into clicks. Low CTR usually means the creative or angle is not landing.

> CTR = Clicks / Impressions x 100%

**CPC (cost per click)** is what you pay for each visitor. It combines CPM and CTR: a better CTR lowers your CPC even if CPM stays the same.

> CPC = Spend / Clicks, or CPM / (CTR x 1,000)

## Revenue metrics

**RPC (revenue per click)** in search arbitrage is the average revenue for each click on a paid listing. It is driven mostly by the vertical, the keywords and the quality of the traffic.

> RPC = Revenue / Paid clicks

**RPV and RPM** measure revenue per visitor or per thousand visitors, across all traffic, including visitors who never click. This is the number to compare with your CPC.

> RPV = Revenue / Visitors, and RPM = RPV x 1,000

**Page CTR** in arbitrage is the share of visitors who click a search term or paid listing on your page. A strong page CTR with a weak RPC often means the keywords are too cheap. A weak page CTR usually means the page does not match the ad.

## Profit metrics

**ROI (return on investment)** tells you how much profit each dollar of spend returns.

> ROI = (Revenue - Spend) / Spend x 100%

**ROAS (return on ad spend)** is revenue divided by spend. E-commerce teams use it a lot. A ROAS of 1.0 means you broke even on media, before any other costs.

> ROAS = Revenue / Spend

## A worked example

You spend $1,000 on TikTok at a $0.10 CPC, which buys 10,000 visitors. 30% of visitors click a related search term, and 40% of those click a paid listing, so you get 1,200 paid clicks. Your average RPC is $1.05.

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Spend | $1,000 |
| Visitors | 10,000 |
| Paid clicks | 1,200 |
| Revenue | $1,260 |
| RPV | $0.126 |
| ROI | 26% |

The campaign works because each visitor earns $0.126 while costing $0.10. If CPC rises to $0.13 as you scale, the same funnel loses money. That is why RPV versus CPC is the line to watch when increasing budgets.

## How to use metrics to scale

- **Decide on the unit you optimize.** For arbitrage it is usually RPV against CPC at the ad set level.
- **Wait for enough data.** Judging an ad after 50 clicks leads to random decisions. Set minimum spend or click thresholds.
- **Watch for delays.** Feed revenue is often reported with a delay and can be adjusted later. Compare final numbers, not only same-day estimates.
- **Scale in steps.** Raise budgets gradually and check that CPC and RPV hold.
- **Track quality.** Sudden jumps in page CTR with falling RPC can signal low-quality traffic. Read more in [why we reject traffic even when the volume looks good](/traffic-providers/why-we-reject-traffic-even-when-the-volume-looks-good).
