**Search arbitrage** is a media buying model where you buy traffic on one platform, send it to a page that shows search results or search-style ads from a feed provider, and earn a share of the revenue when users click those ads. Your profit is the gap between what you pay for a visitor and what that visitor earns you.

It sounds simple, and the mechanics are. Making it profitable, and keeping it compliant, is where the work is. This guide explains how the model works end to end and what separates teams that scale from teams that burn budget.

## How search arbitrage works

A typical flow has four steps:

1. **You buy a click.** Traffic comes from a paid channel such as Meta, TikTok, Taboola, Outbrain or Google.
2. **The visitor lands on your page.** Depending on the setup this is a content article, a search-style landing page or a parked domain.
3. **The page shows monetized search.** A feed partner supplies related search terms or ads. When the user clicks one, they see a results page with paid listings.
4. **You earn when the user clicks a paid listing.** The advertiser pays the ad network, the network pays the feed provider, and the feed provider pays you a revenue share.

Your margin is the revenue per visitor minus the cost per visitor. Everything else in search arbitrage, from creative testing to keyword selection, exists to widen that gap.

## What is RSOC?

**RSOC** stands for *Related Search on Content*. Instead of sending users to a bare search box, an RSOC page is a real article with a block of related search terms placed within it. A reader interested in, say, "best senior dental plans" reads the article, then clicks a related term that leads to a results page with relevant ads.

RSOC became popular with arbitrage teams because it pairs a useful page with monetized search, which is the direction ad networks and feed providers have been pushing the industry. For a media buyer, it changes the job: the article has to be genuinely useful and match the promise of the ad that sent the visitor.

## Where the margin comes from

Three numbers decide whether a campaign makes money:

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| CPC (cost per click) | What you pay the traffic source for each visitor |
| RPC (revenue per click) | What each monetized click on a paid listing earns you |
| RPV or RPM | Revenue per visitor, or per thousand visitors, across all traffic |

A campaign is profitable when revenue per visitor is higher than cost per visitor. You improve it from both sides: buy cheaper, more relevant clicks, and turn more of those visitors into high-value searches. ## Choosing verticals and keywords

Revenue per click depends heavily on how much advertisers pay for the underlying search terms. Verticals such as insurance, finance, health, legal, home services and automotive tend to have more advertiser competition, which usually means higher RPC.

High RPC also attracts more competition from other buyers, so the best opportunities are often:

- **Seasonal angles** such as open enrollment, tax season or back to school
- **Local intent** such as services "near me" in a specific state or city
- **Long-tail problems** that big advertisers bid on but few arbitrage teams target

Always check that the promise in your ad matches the article and the search terms. Mismatched intent means low click-through on the page, low RPC and, eventually, quality complaints.

## Traffic sources

Each traffic source has its own strengths:

- **Meta** offers scale and strong audience targeting, with strict ad review.
- **TikTok** delivers cheap reach and fast creative feedback. Many teams run it through [agency accounts](/guides/tiktok-agency-account-guide) for stability and support.
- **Native networks** like Taboola and Outbrain place ads next to publisher content, a natural fit for RSOC articles.
- **Google and Microsoft search** bring high intent but usually a higher CPC, so margins are thinner.

## Common mistakes that kill accounts

- **Clickbait creative.** Misleading ads get rejected, hurt account health and bring low-quality visitors who never search.
- **Thin pages.** Pages with little content and a wall of search terms rarely pass policy review for long.
- **No tracking.** Without click IDs passed from source to feed report, you cannot see which ad sets make money.
- **Scaling too fast.** Doubling budgets overnight on a winning ad set often resets learning and pushes CPC up.
- **Ignoring traffic quality.** Feed partners watch quality closely. Bot traffic or accidental clicks can lead to clawbacks or a closed account. See [why we reject traffic even when the volume looks good](/traffic-providers/why-we-reject-traffic-even-when-the-volume-looks-good).

## How to get started

1. Get access to a Tier-1 feed partner and understand its content and traffic policies.
2. Set up tracking so each click on a paid listing is attributed to the campaign, ad set and ad.
3. Pick one vertical and one traffic source, and test a handful of angles with small budgets.
4. Kill losers fast, scale winners gradually, and review quality metrics every day.
